How Big Do Havanese Really Get? 71 Owner Weights vs the Breed Standard

Published 2026-10-18

If you have ever stood on the bathroom scale holding your dog and wondered how big do Havanese get compared with the number your breeder quoted, you are not alone. The 7–13 lb figure you see everywhere is real, but it is not the whole story, and a heavier reading does not mean anyone did something wrong. Below we put that figure next to 71 adult weights that owners posted in public threads, and explain where the gap comes from.

Quick answer: The AKC breed profile lists Havanese at 7–13 pounds and 8.5–11.5 inches tall, but the official AKC standard itself sets height limits only, not a weight. Among 71 adult weights that owners shared in two 2026 r/Havanese threads, the median was about 17 lb and roughly two in three dogs weighed more than 13 lb. In the thread that was simply about size (not about "big" dogs), the median was 13 lb. Height at the withers, frame, and your vet’s assessment tell you more than the scale alone.

How big do Havanese get according to the breed standards?

On paper, a Havanese is a toy dog roughly 8.5 to 11.5 inches tall at the shoulder. The weight figure depends on which kennel club you read, and the AKC’s own written standard does not contain one at all.

The 7–13 lb number comes from a profile, not the standard text

The AKC breed page summarizes the Havanese as 8.5–11.5 inches and 7–13 pounds. When you open the official AKC Havanese standard (PDF), the "Size, Proportion, and Substance" section gives an ideal height of 9 to 10½ inches and an acceptable range of 8½ to 11½ inches. A dog outside that range is disqualified, except that the minimum does not apply to dogs under twelve months. The standard describes the dog as moderately boned, "never coarse or fragile", and slightly longer than tall. It never states a weight.

That detail matters for owners who feel they were misled. A breeder who says "Havanese are 7–13 pounds" is repeating a convenient summary. Judges in an AKC ring measure height, not weight.

Other registries draw the lines differently

The United Kennel Club Havanese standard lists a height of 8½ to 10½ inches and a weight of 7 to 14 pounds, "with mid size much preferred", and states that height limits are more important than weight. It is also the only one of these documents that lists weight under 7 or over 14 pounds as a disqualification. That explains why you see "7–14 lb" on some sites and "7–13 lb" on others.

The FCI standard No. 250, used across most of Europe, gives height only: 23 to 27 cm at the withers, with a tolerance from 21 to 29 cm (about 8.3 to 11.4 inches). It says nothing about pounds or kilograms.

What do real adult Havanese weigh?

In owner reports, a lot more than 13 lb. Of 71 adult weights we tallied, 24 fell inside 7–13 lb, 4 were between 13 and 14 lb, and 43 were above 14 lb, with a range of 7 to 29 lb.

How we collected the numbers

We read two public r/Havanese threads from 2026: "Is 20lbs too big?" (September 2026, 87 comments) and "Havi size" (June 2026, 73 comments). We counted every comment that gave a current adult weight. We left out puppies under about a year, a dog described as a Havanese–poodle mix, and one dog its owner described as overweight. Kilograms were converted to pounds (1 kg = 2.2 lb), and a range such as "16 to 18 lb" was entered as its midpoint.

These are self-reported numbers from volunteers, not a survey. Some dogs may be mixes, some owners round, and nobody measured height. Treat the tally as a reality check on the 7–13 lb figure, not as a new breed statistic.

The thread title changes the answer

Look at the two columns. A thread called "Is 20lbs too big?" naturally pulls in owners of larger dogs who want to say "mine too". The neutral "Havi size" thread produced a median of 13 lb, right at the top of the profile range, and more than half its dogs were inside 7–13 lb. The honest reading is that many Havanese do sit inside the familiar numbers, and a sizeable minority do not.

Owner comments also show how often a big Havanese runs in the family. One owner wrote that their 3-year-old male is 17.2 lb and his sire is 18 lb. Another reported a 19 lb dog whose sire is 22 lb and whose dam is about 8 lb. Several said their dog was "the biggest of the litter" from day one.

Why do so many Havanese outgrow the 7–13 lb figure?

Because the breed is defined by height and sturdiness, not by a weight cap, and a sturdy dog near the top of the height range can easily weigh more than 13 lb while still matching the written standard.

Taller-but-legal dogs weigh more

In a 2021 Showsight Magazine article on the Havanese standard, a breeder-judge describes their personal ideal as about 1.25 to 1.5 pounds per inch of height. By that rule, an 8.5-inch dog would be about 10 to 12 pounds, and an 11.5-inch dog could be 14 to 17 pounds and "still be considered small and sturdy". The article also stresses that the 8.5-inch dog should not be fine-boned and the 11.5-inch dog should not be heavy-boned.

Put that next to the profile range and the contradiction is obvious: a Havanese at the tallest height the AKC allows can sit above 13 lb without being "wrong" for the breed. That is not an official formula, and we use it in the checker only as a rough comparison point.

The coat hides the frame

A Havanese in full coat looks larger and rounder than it is, so owners often guess wrong in both directions. Your hands are a better guide than your eyes. If you keep your dog in a full coat, the brushing session from our at-home grooming schedule is a natural moment to feel the frame under the hair, and during a coat change the outline can look different week to week while the dog’s size has not changed at all.

Breeding goals vary

Breeders choose where in the range they aim. The UKC wording prefers "mid size", while the Showsight author notes that breeders and judges may have size preferences but cannot reward an under- or over-sized dog. Pet-line puppies are not bred to stay inside any show band. If one of the parents is on the large side, a large puppy is not a surprise.

See show-standard Havanese in the ring

Seeing the dogs judged against the standard helps you calibrate. In this Westminster Kennel Club video from February 2026, you can watch the Havanese breed judging, where the judge assesses the entries on breed standard, movement, and presentation.

Show dogs are a selected group presented in full coat, so the video shows what the standard looks like in practice, not what an average family Havanese weighs.

Is my Havanese too big if it weighs 20 pounds?

Not automatically. A 20 lb Havanese is above every published weight figure, but several owners in the threads reported 19–26 lb dogs that, they said, their vets were happy with. Height, frame, and body condition decide whether the number is a problem, and only your vet can judge that.

Separate "outside the show range" from "unhealthy"

A dog can be above the UKC weight disqualification and still be a well-built companion. Show limits answer the question "can this dog compete?", not "is this dog okay?". Two commenters made exactly this point: the standard matters if you show, while a healthy companion is judged by a vet’s exam.

Is it my fault my Havanese is bigger than the breeder said?

Usually not. Adult size is shaped largely by parents and frame, which you did not choose after bringing the puppy home. If you have been quietly blaming yourself or doubting the breeder’s honesty, the evidence above gives you permission to stop. The productive step is not guilt but a measurement and a conversation with your vet.

That same shift helps with other breed claims you read online.

How to measure and track your Havanese’s size

Weigh consistently, measure height at the withers on a flat floor, and record both together. Two numbers give a far clearer picture than one.

  1. Weigh the same way each time. Use a pet scale, or weigh yourself, then yourself holding the dog, and subtract. Weigh at a similar time of day.
  2. Find the withers. That is the highest point of the shoulder blades, just behind the neck. Part the coat so you are touching the dog, not the hair.
  3. Stand the dog square. Use a level, non-slip surface with the head in a natural position, not stretched up toward a treat.
  4. Measure straight up. Hold a ruler upright beside the front leg and lay a flat object (a book or a spirit level) across the withers. Read the height where it meets the ruler.
  5. Repeat three times and average. Wiggly dogs produce different readings; an average smooths that out.
  6. Write it down with the date. Note weight, height, and coat length. For a puppy, a monthly entry is enough.
  7. Bring the log to your vet. Ask what weight they would consider a good target for your dog’s frame.

Puppies are a moving target

The AKC height minimum is waived for dogs under twelve months, and our tally excluded puppies for the same reason: they are still growing. One owner in the "Havi size" thread said both of their dogs were "95% done growing at 6 months", but that is one household’s experience. If your puppy already outweighs the breeder’s estimate, wait until adulthood before deciding where it will land. While you wait, focus on what you can actually shape, such as calm alone time from our puppy independence guide.

Common mistakes when judging Havanese size

Most worry comes from comparing the wrong numbers. These are the errors that show up again and again in owner discussions.

  • Treating 7–13 lb as a rule. It is a profile summary; the AKC standard text uses height.
  • Comparing with one dog online. A 9 lb Havanese and a 24 lb Havanese can both be typical of their own lines.
  • Judging by the coat. Long hair adds visual bulk. Feel the frame instead.
  • Weighing a puppy against adult figures. Under twelve months, the standards themselves make allowances.
  • Cutting food based on the breed number alone. Any change to how much your dog eats is a question for your vet, not for a breed chart.
  • Assuming a big Havanese is "not purebred". Several owners who described their dogs as purebred or full-blooded reported adults over 20 lb; size alone does not prove or disprove ancestry.

A short self-check before you worry

Ask yourself three questions: Do I know my dog’s height? Do I know the parents’ sizes? Has my vet commented on body condition? If the answers are no, you are worrying with incomplete data. Getting those three answers is the most useful thing you can do this week.

Frequently asked questions

How big do Havanese get as adults?

The AKC breed profile lists 8.5–11.5 inches and 7–13 pounds. In 71 adult weights shared by owners in two 2026 Reddit threads, weights ranged from 7 to 29 lb with a median of about 17 lb, so many companions are larger than the profile figure.

Does the AKC standard set a weight limit for Havanese?

No. The official AKC standard sets height: an ideal of 9 to 10½ inches and an acceptable range of 8½ to 11½ inches. The 7–13 lb figure appears on the AKC breed profile page, not in the standard text.

Why do some sites say 7–14 pounds?

The United Kennel Club standard lists 7 to 14 pounds and disqualifies dogs under 7 or over 14 pounds. Sites quoting 7–14 lb are usually following the UKC figure, while 7–13 lb follows the AKC profile.

Is a 20 lb Havanese purebred?

Weight alone cannot tell you. In the threads we reviewed, several owners who described their dogs as purebred reported adults over 20 lb. Pedigree papers or the breeder’s records answer ancestry questions better than a scale.

How tall should a Havanese be?

AKC: ideal 9 to 10½ inches, acceptable 8½ to 11½ inches. UKC: 8½ to 10½ inches. FCI: 23 to 27 cm with a tolerance from 21 to 29 cm. Measure at the withers with the dog standing square.

When does a Havanese stop growing?

The sources we reviewed do not give a fixed age. The AKC standard waives the height minimum for dogs under twelve months, which is why adult comparisons start at a year. Your vet can track growth at routine visits.

The bottom line on Havanese size

So, how big do Havanese get? Officially, 8.5 to 11.5 inches tall, with the 7–13 lb figure as a profile summary rather than a rule. In real homes, many healthy adults weigh 14 to 25 lb, and a bigger dog is not evidence that you or your breeder failed. Measure the height, keep a simple log, ask your vet about body condition, and let the number on the scale be one data point instead of a verdict.
