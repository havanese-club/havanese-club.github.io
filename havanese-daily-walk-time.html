How Far Do Havanese Really Walk? Real Owner Daily Walk Times and Distances vs Breed-Guide Advice

26 owners shared their daily routines. Their median is longer than most breed guides suggest, and the miles are shorter than you might think.

Home›Blog›Havanese Daily Walks

Published: 2026-11-03 · Havanese Club · Sofia Navarro

If you have wondered how far Havanese really walk in everyday homes, the honest answer is: less far than the minutes suggest, and longer than most breed guides say. Breed guides tend to quote 20 to 30 minutes a day. Owners who described their routine in a busy 2026 community thread walked a median of about 50 minutes, often at a slow, sniffing pace that covers barely two miles an hour. You do not need a marathon dog or a perfect schedule to give your Havanese a good day, and this guide shows you why.

Quick answer: Breed-club and charity guides list about 20–30 minutes of exercise a day for a Havanese. In 26 owner routines we tallied, daily walking time ranged from about 12 to 210 minutes, with a median of 50 minutes, usually split into two to four outings. Distances were modest: owners who gave both time and distance covered roughly 1 mile in 30 minutes and 1.5 miles in 40 minutes. Several owners said their dog simply does not care for long walks and gets its exercise in the yard or the house instead.

How long do Havanese owners actually walk each day?

The typical owner in our tally walks a Havanese about 50 minutes a day. Half of the routines sit between roughly 38 and 71 minutes, and almost all of them are split into more than one outing.

Where the numbers come from

In October 2026 an owner asked “How long do you walk your havanese for each day?” in the r/Havanese community. The thread drew 87 comments. We read all 56 that were still visible and kept every reply that gave a clear daily walking time: 26 owners. When someone gave a range, such as “20–30 minutes,” we used the middle. When someone said “several” short walks, we counted two. Replies that only gave miles, or said “it depends,” went into the distance and no-walk sections below instead. One Havanese-poodle mix was left out.

So 22 of the 26 owners walked at least 30 minutes a day. The shortest routine was about 12 minutes; the longest, an earlier dog who went out three or four times a day for an hour with the owner’s dad, was about 3.5 hours.

How owners split the day

The most common pattern was not one big walk. It was a mix: short potty trips, one or two “real” walks, and play at home. One owner described a 40-minute fast walk first thing, then several 10–15 minute “snifaris” where the dog sets the pace. An apartment owner did two short potty walks, a 30-minute walk and a one-hour walk. If you are still working out where potty trips end and walks begin, our guide to moving a Havanese from the potty tray to outdoor breaks explains why separating the two helps.

What do breed guides recommend for Havanese exercise?

Most published guides put the Havanese at about 20 to 30 minutes of exercise a day. That is a reasonable minimum, but it is not a ceiling, and it describes exercise rather than walking alone.

Dogs NSW also describes the breed as “muscular and lively with great stamina,” which matches the owners in the thread whose dogs ran three miles or wanted to keep going after six. The guides are a floor you can build on, not a limit you must respect.

Why do real owners walk longer than the guides suggest?

Because walking time and exercise are not the same thing. Much of a Havanese walk is sniffing, standing and checking “pee-mail,” so owners spend more minutes outside to give the same amount of movement.

Sniffing takes time

Owner after owner described a slow, nose-down dog. One owner said their dogs sniff and mark “over and over, every few feet.” Another joked that a 2 km walk takes three days because of side quests. The top-voted reply in the thread said a slow 40-minute sniffing walk tires her dog out just as much as a three-mile walk. That is one owner’s experience, but it explains why long minutes do not always mean long miles.

Potty trips count as walks in apartments

Several apartment owners said every bathroom trip is a small walk. Four short trips add up quickly, so their daily totals look high even though no single outing is long.

Some dogs simply ask for more

One owner calls their dog “Adventure Pup.” Another walks two miles in the morning and another one or two miles later in the day. If your Havanese is still full of energy after the guide’s 30 minutes, you are not doing something wrong by giving more.

How far do Havanese walk in miles?

Usually one to two miles a day, at an easy pace of about two miles an hour. A minority of active dogs cover three to six miles.

The takeaway: plan your Havanese walk in minutes, not miles. A dog that sniffs a lot may only cover a mile in half an hour, and that is completely normal for this breed in real homes.

Is it okay if my Havanese doesn’t like long walks?

Yes, many owners in the thread said the same. Roughly five of the 56 replies described dogs that skip regular walks and get their exercise in the yard, the house or a dog park instead.

One owner said their two boys “don’t really care for walks” and sometimes turn back before reaching the dog park 1/8 mile away. Another only takes their 11-pound dog when the dog clearly wants to go. A farm Havanese follows its owner around the yard instead of going on walks. One small 5 kg dog goes on a 15–20 minute sniff walk “under protest,” although she can manage much longer adventures. Most of these owners did not sound worried.

What these homes have in common is daily movement of some kind: zoomies in the yard, running games in the house, fetch, or hallway laps in an apartment building when the weather is rough. If your dog would rather play than walk, that still counts. A calm, well-exercised Havanese is often a quieter one at the window, too; our plan for window and doorbell barking pairs well with a steady daily routine.

When a dog that loved walks starts saying no

Owners also noted that walking habits change over time. One owner’s dog did about a mile when young and later preferred shorter walks; a 14-year-old now takes a single slow lap around the block. If a change is sudden or comes with other changes in your dog, check in with your vet.

How to build a Havanese walk routine, step by step

You can do this in a week. Start where you are, then adjust using what your own dog tells you.

  1. Write down today’s real routine. Count every outing, including short potty trips. Use the checker at the top to see where you sit.
  2. Pick one anchor walk. Choose the time your dog has the most energy. Many owners use the morning. Make this your longest walk, 20–40 minutes.
  3. Add short sniff walks. Two or three 10–15 minute outings where your dog chooses the direction and sniffs freely.
  4. Add play on busy days. Ten minutes of fetch or zoomies in the yard or the hallway can replace a walk when time or weather is short.
  5. Watch your dog, not the clock. Signs of a good routine: your dog settles to nap after walks, and it is not restless in the evening. One owner found that four walks, starting with two miles, helped her dog sleep through the night.
  6. Adjust once a week. Add or remove ten minutes and see what changes. Small steps are easier to stick with.
  7. Look after the coat after walks. Long fur collects leaves, burrs and damp. A quick check fits into your at-home Havanese grooming schedule.

Common walk-routine mistakes

Most mistakes come from copying a number instead of watching the dog.

  • Counting miles instead of minutes. A sniffing Havanese might cover just one mile in 30 minutes. That is a full walk, not a failure.
  • Rushing every walk. Several owners said sniffing tires their dog out. If every walk is brisk, add a sniff walk.
  • Feeling guilty about short walks. One owner in the thread said the comments made them “feel guilty” about their one 20–30 minute walk, then added that the dog seems content. Content counts.
  • Forcing a reluctant dog. If your Havanese hides when the leash comes out, try shorter outings, a new route, or play at home before you push for distance.
  • Skipping the after-walk coat check. Damp fur tangles quickly. Our guide to preventing mats during the coat change explains why wet coats need extra attention.
  • Comparing to the wrong dog. Havanese vary in size and energy. Our tally of real Havanese weights compared with the breed standard shows how wide the range is, and energy varies just as much.

FAQ

How long should I walk my Havanese each day?

Breed guides list about 20–30 minutes of exercise a day. In 26 owner routines we tallied, the median was about 50 minutes of walking, usually split into two to four outings. Use the guides as a minimum and your dog’s energy as your guide.

How far can a Havanese walk?

Most owners reported one to two miles a day at a relaxed pace of about two miles an hour. Some active Havanese walk three to four miles a day, and one owner reported a six-mile outing.

Is one walk a day enough for a Havanese?

For some dogs, yes. Several owners do one 20–40 minute walk plus yard or house play and describe content dogs. Most owners, though, split the day into two or more outings.

Do sniff walks count as exercise?

Owners in the thread count them. The top-voted reply said a slow 40-minute sniffing walk tires her dog as much as a three-mile walk. Mix sniff walks with brisker ones to suit your dog.

My Havanese hates walks. What should I do?

You are not alone; several owners said the same. Keep walks short and let your dog choose the route, then give daily play in the yard or home. If a dog that used to love walks suddenly refuses them, check in with your vet.

Can a Havanese go running with me?

Some do. One owner said their Havanese runs three miles with them a couple of times a week at about a 9:30-per-mile pace. Build up gradually and let your dog set the limits.

The bottom line: you already know your dog best

Breed guides give you a starting point of 20–30 minutes. Real Havanese owners walk a median of about 50 minutes, usually in short, sniff-filled outings that add up to a mile or two. Somewhere in that range is the routine that fits your dog and your life. Use the checker, try one change this week, and trust what your Havanese shows you. You’ve got this.
